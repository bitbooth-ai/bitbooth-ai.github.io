Feature · 01

Portrait of a bear,
in almond

They arrive in small boxes and cause immediate meetings. Someone must decide who gets the bear. The character macarons of Cheeky Bite — bears, lemons, and a rotating cast of seasonal guests — have the rare quality of being too charming to eat and too delicious not to.

Each face is piped by hand, one steady breath at a time. The eyebrows, our correspondent can confirm, are sometimes redone four times. It shows. No two are in the same mood, and neither are their new owners.

Feature · 03

The garden you're
allowed to eat

Peonies that never wilt, dahlias with no vase requirements, roses piped petal by petal until botany gives up and applauds. The buttercream flower cakes are the house's grand act — florist's-window beauty with a crumb underneath.

Our advice, after extensive field research: order the full bouquet box, put it in the middle of the table, and watch a room full of adults refuse to cut it first.

The Interview · 04

One woman,
ten thousand petals

Every face, every petal — really just you?

Just me. My piping bag and I have an understanding.

The look choup fruit sweets fool everyone. Is that the point?

It's an old Thai craft — sweets shaped as fruit so generosity looks as good as it tastes. The gasp when someone realises? That's the point.

Describe your ideal customer.

Someone celebrating something. Or someone who has decided to. Tuesday counts.

And your artistic philosophy, in a sentence?

Sweet as art — but art you get to eat, so better.
