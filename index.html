Cheeky Bite —
twenty design directions

One bakery, twenty candidate websites. Same photography, same products — character macarons, Thai sweets, buttercream flower cakes. Eight earlier directions were culled (dark themes, text-over-photo readability, joyless palettes — see PRODUCT.md for reasons). The third batch (16–20) throws away the shared skeleton: a board game, an operating system, a swatch book, a field guide, a chat thread. The fourth batch (22–30) aims for high-end class kept warm — luxury-world structures (a tasting menu, a wedding suite, an auction catalogue, a glossy magazine, a grand hotel) that still sell happiness. Everything below is light, joyful, dark-on-light readable, and built for phones. Click through and pick a lane.

A Victorian naturalist's taxonomy — Paeonia cremora, Ursus macaron — with numbered plates, hand annotations, and a stamped Specimen Request Form.

Sweet Talk

The whole site is a chat thread with the baker. Photo messages with tappable reactions, a voice note, quick replies, and an in-chat call-me-back card.

 → 22

The Tasting Menu

Seven courses on cream card stock. Centered fine-dining typography, plates that bloom into view, pairing notes like "pairs well with Sunday mornings". Course VII is the order form, served by telephone.

 → 24

The Invitation Suite

A luxury wedding-stationery flat-lay: opening envelope with wax seal, deckle-edged cards, ribbon-banded menu, taped photo prints — and the order form is an RSVP card filled in "by pen".

 → 25

Looks Like / Tastes Like

Two senses per product: the sight face (photo + color story) flips to the taste face (a flavor poem, notes staggering in). "Looks like art. Tastes like a good day."

 → 26

Lot & Provenance

A deadpan auction catalogue — Sale 001: Sweetness & Light. Lots with medium, provenance ("piped this morning") and estimates ("priceless — enquire"). Register a paddle to order.

 → 27

The Sweet Issue

A glossy magazine: SWEET masthead cover with cover lines, contents page, drop-cap features, a pull quote, the baker interviewed, and the order form hidden in the back-page classifieds.

 → 28

The House of Small Treasures

Heritage luxury, English voice: gold rules that bloom from center, lotus-scalloped treasure frames, gold-leaf shimmer on hover, and an order form styled as "requesting an audience".

 → 29

The Porcelain Room

A warm little museum: products inside gilt-rimmed porcelain plates hung salon-style, brass caption plaques ("Attribution disputed: not actually fruit"), and an Acquisitions Desk to order.

Le Grand Hôtel Cheeky

A belle-époque grand hotel with a staff of one. Three-tier tea-stand hero that assembles on load, floors instead of sections, a ringable concierge bell, and a door-hanger order card.
